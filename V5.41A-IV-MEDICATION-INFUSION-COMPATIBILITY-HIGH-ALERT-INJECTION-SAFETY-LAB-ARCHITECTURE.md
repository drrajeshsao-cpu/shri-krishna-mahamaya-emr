# Mahamaya EMR V5.41A — IV Medication Administration • Infusion • Compatibility • High-Alert Injection Safety Intelligence Lab

**Build:** 2026.08.15-R26A  
**Dataset:** MTLPI 2.5A

## Purpose
A clinician/pharmacy-reviewed safety workspace for injectable medicines. It connects order/product verification, preparation, pump programming, vascular-access/line checks, compatibility verification, high-alert safeguards, extravasation recognition and transitions of care without pretending that a static app can safely calculate or authorize IV administration parameters.

## Core safety architecture
1. **Exact product first:** generic name alone is insufficient for formulation, concentration, diluent, route, stability or infusion-rate decisions.
2. **No guessed compatibility:** Y-site/admixture compatibility must be verified for exact products, concentrations, diluents and conditions in a current institution-approved source.
3. **High-alert safeguards:** ISMP high-alert categories receive explicit independent-check/system-barrier prompts.
4. **Pump ≠ authorization:** smart-pump/DERS limits are a safety barrier, not proof that the order, concentration or route is correct.
5. **Line-to-patient trace:** bag/syringe → tubing → channel → lumen → patient verification is built into high-risk workflows.
6. **Extravasation stop-and-assess:** no automatic flush or antidote dose is generated; agent-specific local protocol is required.
7. **Injection infection prevention:** CDC safe-injection and aseptic-practice principles are part of the medication-safety workflow.
8. **Transitions are deliberate:** bag/syringe changes, transport, handoff, interruption and restart trigger re-verification.

## Scientific boundary
No automatic IV dose, dilution, concentration, infusion or IV-push rate, compatibility verdict, Y-site decision, stability/beyond-use time, line assignment, extravasation antidote, pump setting or high-alert override is generated. Current product labeling, pharmacist/institutional references, device instructions and patient-specific clinical review remain mandatory.
